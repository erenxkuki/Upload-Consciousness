# Consciousness Theories (research module)

For each theory: central claims, scientific support, criticisms, unresolved questions,
computational implications, and the gap between theoretical compatibility and
empirical proof. No theory is treated as proof of machine consciousness.

## Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT)

- **Central claims:** Consciousness arises when information is broadcast across a
  global workspace of interconnected brain regions, making it available to multiple
  cognitive processes.
- **Support:** Neuroimaging correlates of conscious access; masking experiments.
- **Criticisms:** Hard to measure "broadcast"; unclear why broadcast yields
  subjectivity.
- **Computational implications:** A system with a shared blackboard/broadcast
  layer could be *compatible* with GNWT, but compatibility is not evidence.
- **Gap:** No empirical test that a broadcast layer produces experience.

## Integrated Information Theory (IIT)

- **Central claims:** Consciousness corresponds to integrated information (Phi);
  systems with high Phi are conscious.
- **Support:** Mathematical formalism; some neural correlates.
- **Criticisms:** Phi is hard to compute; panpsychist implications; IIT as
  pseudoscience debate.
- **Computational implications:** Could motivate measuring integration in
  architectures, but Phi is not a consciousness detector.
- **Gap:** Phi has not been validated as subjective experience.

## Higher-Order Theories (HOT)

- **Central claims:** A mental state is conscious when there is a higher-order
  representation of it.
- **Support:** Introspection and metacognition research.
- **Criticisms:** Regress problems; unclear neural basis.
- **Computational implications:** Self-model and reflection modules are
  structurally compatible; not evidence.
- **Gap:** Higher-order representation does not establish experience.

## Predictive Processing

- **Central claims:** The brain minimizes prediction error; perception and action
  are inference.
- **Support:** Broad explanatory power; active inference.
- **Criticisms:** Risk of being unfalsifiable; scope creep.
- **Computational implications:** Natural fit for belief updating and learning
  modules; not consciousness.
- **Gap:** Predictive processing is a framework, not a consciousness proof.

## Recurrent Processing Theory

- **Central claims:** Consciousness requires recurrent (feedback) processing, not
  just feedforward.
- **Support:** Visual awareness studies.
- **Criticisms:** Recurrence alone may be insufficient.
- **Computational implications:** Architectures with feedback loops compatible;
  not evidence.

## Embodied Cognition

- **Central claims:** Cognition is shaped by the body and environment.
- **Support:** Sensorimotor studies.
- **Criticisms:** Vague boundaries; hard to test.
- **Computational implications:** Embodiment platform relevant for testing
  self-model coherence; not proof.

## Whole-Brain Emulation research

- **Central claims:** Replicating neural structure at sufficient resolution could
  preserve mental function.
- **Support:** Theoretical; connectomics progress.
- **Criticisms:** Unknown required resolution; substrate questions; ethics.
- **Computational implications:** Our architecture is not WBE; it is a
  higher-level cognitive representation. No claim of brain emulation.

## Conclusion

No theory validates machine consciousness. Theories inform architecture design
but do not certify experience. Behavioural benchmarks are not consciousness
detectors.
